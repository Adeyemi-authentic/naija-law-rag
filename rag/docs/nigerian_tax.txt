Nigerian Federal Tax: Key Provisions (Personal Income Tax, Companies Income Tax, VAT).
Status: reflects the Nigeria Tax Act 2025 (NTA), which took effect on 1 January 2026 and
consolidated and replaced the former Personal Income Tax Act, Companies Income Tax Act,
and Value Added Tax Act. This is a plain-language reference summary of the main rules with
their figures as at 2026. It is legal and tax information only, not advice; consult the
Nigeria Revenue Service (NRS) or a professional for any specific situation.

Personal Income Tax (PIT): who pays. An individual who is resident in Nigeria is taxed on
worldwide income. For employment income, tax applies where the individual is resident in
Nigeria and performs duties wholly or partly in Nigeria. Personal income tax on employees
is collected by employers through the Pay As You Earn (PAYE) system.

Personal Income Tax: minimum-wage exemption. Employees who earn no more than the national
minimum wage (70,000 naira) are no longer liable to personal income tax or to deduction of
monthly PAYE.

Personal Income Tax: annual tax bands and rates (2026). The first 800,000 naira of annual
taxable income is taxed at 0 percent. The next 2,200,000 naira is taxed at 15 percent. The
next 9,000,000 naira is taxed at 18 percent. The next 13,000,000 naira is taxed at 21
percent. The next 25,000,000 naira is taxed at 23 percent. Any income above 50,000,000
naira is taxed at 25 percent. The expanded 0 percent band replaces the previous 1 percent
minimum tax rule.

Personal Income Tax: rent relief. A rent relief is allowed of 20 percent of annual rent
paid, up to a maximum of 500,000 naira. This replaces the former consolidated relief
allowance under the Personal Income Tax Act.

Personal Income Tax: termination benefits. Compensation for loss of employment is a
chargeable gain only to the extent it exceeds 50,000,000 naira (raised from the former
10,000,000 naira threshold).

Companies Income Tax (CIT): small company rate. A small company, defined as a company with
annual turnover not exceeding 50,000,000 naira and fixed assets below 250,000,000 naira,
pays companies income tax at 0 percent. Companies that provide professional services are
excluded from this small-company relief.

Companies Income Tax: standard rate. The rate of income tax for any Nigerian company other
than a small company is 30 percent. The Act allows for a later reduction to 25 percent from
a date to be specified in an order issued by the President on the advice of the National
Economic Council.

Development levy. The Act introduces a single 4 percent development levy on the assessable
profits of companies, replacing several earmarked taxes such as the tertiary education tax
and the Nigeria Police Trust Fund levy. Small companies and non-resident companies are
excluded from the development levy.

Value Added Tax (VAT): rate. The standard rate of VAT is 7.5 percent. This rate has applied
since 1 February 2020, when it was increased from 5 percent.

Value Added Tax: zero-rated supplies. The following are taxed at 0 percent: basic food
items on a defined list, medical products and services, pharmaceutical products, books and
educational materials, exported services, goods and services purchased by diplomats, and
goods purchased for use in humanitarian donor-funded projects.

Value Added Tax: exempt supplies. Plant and machinery for use in an export processing zone
or a free trade zone are exempt from VAT.

Value Added Tax: input VAT recovery. Businesses may claim input VAT on services and on
capital assets such as fixed-asset purchases, not only on goods and materials for
production or resale. A reverse-charge mechanism applies to services for which no tax
invoice was received.
